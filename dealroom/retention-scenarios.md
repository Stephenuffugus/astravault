# Retention Scenarios — Astra Vault

How Stephen can monetize the asset while retaining rights. This is the playbook for the question he actually asked: "can i hand it to someone and sell the idea or sell myself while retaining some or most rights."

The short answer: **yes, across multiple deal shapes**, and the architecture of the patent portfolio (floor + ceiling, see `patent-portfolio.md`) makes this structurally easier than it would be otherwise.

## The seven deal shapes, ranked by retention

### 1. License the SWS protocol non-exclusively, per vertical
**Retention:** Maximum. Stephen retains title to every patent. Continues operating Astra Vault. Continues filing future applications.
**Typical structure:** A non-astronomy company (fitness, reading, music, social) wants to add attention-economy mechanics. Stephen grants a non-exclusive license to use Patent #0 (the SWS protocol) in that specific vertical, for a defined duration, in defined territories.
**Typical economics:** $50k-$500k upfront + 1-3% royalty on attention-economy-derived revenue. Multiple licensees possible across multiple verticals.
**Best for:** Inbound interest from non-astronomy attention-economy projects. Brave alumni, Sweatcoin alumni, Pi Network ecosystem.
**Pros:** Truly retains everything. Builds a recurring royalty stream. Each new license is pure margin once the legal template is built.
**Cons:** Each conversation is slow. Total upside is bounded by per-license size × number of licensees.
**Likely total value over 5 years:** $200k-$3M cumulative.

### 2. Sell Astra Vault application patents (#1, #4, #5) + the LLC + app, keep Patent #0
**Retention:** Strong. Stephen keeps Patent #0 (the platform-level floor). Sells the astronomy-vertical-specific stack.
**Typical structure:** A strategic acquirer (ZWO, Vaonis, Unistellar, Meta Reality Labs, a smart-glasses-incumbent) wants Astra Vault as a product play. They get the LLC + app + 3 application patents + the open-source library + the 5 outreach drafts. Stephen retains Patent #0 and grants them a perpetual non-exclusive license to use Patent #0 specifically within the acquired Astra Vault product.
**Typical economics:** $3M-$15M upfront cash + equity in acquirer + Stephen as advisor/Director for 18-36 months at $200-400k/year.
**Best for:** A buyer who wants the astronomy product without the broader protocol baggage.
**Pros:** Stephen monetizes the Astra Vault-specific work. Patent #0 stays his and continues to generate revenue.
**Cons:** Once Astra Vault is sold, Stephen typically can't compete in the astronomy vertical for 2-5 years (non-compete). Loses the day-to-day creative project.
**Likely outcome:** This is the most realistic "real exit" path within 18-36 months.

### 3. Acquihire + Director-of-X role
**Retention:** Moderate. Stephen joins the acquirer; assets transfer; he retains some equity + a senior role.
**Typical structure:** A larger company (think Meta Reality Labs, Apple Hardware, Snap, Niantic, an XR platform) acqui-hires the LLC. Stephen becomes "Director of Attention Protocols" or "Head of Astronomy AR" or similar. 2-4 year cliff on retention equity.
**Typical economics:** $1M-$10M total package (cash + stock + salary, mostly back-loaded). Sometimes Stephen retains a small founder royalty on the underlying patents (1-3%).
**Best for:** Big-tech tailwind moment. Sounds like more cash but is structurally a long-term employment contract.
**Pros:** Resources to actually ship at scale. Job security. Title.
**Cons:** Stephen works for someone else. Equity grant typically vests 4 years and is at the acquirer's stock price, not Astra Vault's.
**Likely value over 4 years (vesting):** $500k-$4M after taxes.

### 4. Spin out Astra Vault as a NewCo + raise external capital
**Retention:** Stephen retains control + significant ownership (~30-50% post-Series A).
**Typical structure:** Stephen rolls just the Astra Vault assets (LLC, app, 3 application patents, open-source) into a NewCo. Licenses Patent #0 from his existing LLC to the NewCo perpetually. Raises a seed/Series A from a venture investor.
**Typical economics:** Seed raise of $500k-$2M at a $4-10M pre-money valuation → Stephen retains ~70% post-seed. Series A of $3-10M at $15-40M post-money → Stephen retains ~30-45% post-Series A.
**Best for:** Smart glasses tailwind is real and Stephen wants to scale this himself.
**Pros:** Largest theoretical upside ($50-500M outcomes possible if smart glasses hit). Stephen stays operator. Patent #0 still his.
**Cons:** Raises take 6-12 months. Investors want growth metrics. Dilutive. Stephen has a board and reports up.
**Likely 5-year outcome distribution:** 10% chance $20M+ exit, 30% chance $5-20M exit, 60% chance struggles/winds down with founder retaining patents.

### 5. Patent-only outright sale to a strategic
**Retention:** Low. Stephen sells all four patents (#0 + the 3 new ones) to a strategic buyer. Keeps the LLC + app + open-source but no patent protection.
**Typical structure:** A patent-savvy strategic (a big-tech, a patent-holding company) wants the portfolio. Stephen walks with cash.
**Typical economics:** $1M-$10M lump sum.
**Best for:** Stephen wants to be done.
**Pros:** Single check, clean exit.
**Cons:** Loses everything that makes future ventures defensible. Doesn't continue creative work.
**Why I don't recommend this:** Stephen's identity is the inventor; selling the inventor identity is irreversible.

### 6. Patent monetization shop / "patent troll" route
**Retention:** Title remains with Stephen but revenue is split.
**Typical structure:** Partner with Acacia / Marathon Patent Group / RPX / a patent monetization firm. They sue infringers (real or speculative) on commission. Stephen gets 30-70% of any settlement / license revenue.
**Typical economics:** $0 unless they secure licenses, then 30-70% of $100k-$5M per settlement.
**Best for:** Patents Stephen isn't actively practicing on.
**Pros:** Free upside.
**Cons:** **Reputation cost.** Closes doors at every major tech company. Astra Vault's partnership pipeline dies. The astronomy community is small and will know.
**Why I don't recommend this:** the reputation cost outweighs the dollars.

### 7. Hold-and-build + bootstrap
**Retention:** Maximum. Stephen keeps everything.
**Typical structure:** Stephen builds Astra Vault into a real business himself. Premium tier subscription, hardware accessory product, paid integrations.
**Typical economics:** $50-500k/year revenue at year 3 (the "real business" scenario from earlier). Profitable owner-operator business.
**Best for:** Stephen has other income and can put 18-24 months into building before needing the business to support him.
**Pros:** Full control. Full upside if it hits. Optionality preserved — can always do any of the other deal shapes later from a position of strength.
**Cons:** Slow. Lonely. Slow.
**Likely outcome:** Solid lifestyle business if Stephen sticks. Most likely failure mode is burnout, not market failure.

## My honest recommendation

A **layered strategy** that maximizes optionality:

1. **Months 0-3:** File the 3 provisional patents. Publish `@astravault/alpaca-client` to npm. Send the ZWO + Unistellar + AstroBin outreach. Soft-launch Astra Vault to a closed beta (100 friends-and-family users).

2. **Months 3-9:** Quiet conversations with 3-5 strategic candidates (#2 deal shape). Track who's interested. Don't propose, listen.

3. **Months 9-12:** Either:
   - One of the strategics is interested → push toward deal shape #2 (sell application patents + LLC, keep Patent #0)
   - Smart glasses traction is materializing → push toward deal shape #4 (spin out + raise)
   - Nothing biting → go to deal shape #7 (bootstrap), reassess at month 18

This sequence costs Stephen ~$30-50k (patent attorney + LLC maintenance + minor app spend) over 12 months, and at the end he either has a real exit conversation or a real business — never both gone.

## What he must NOT do

- **Don't sell Patent #0.** It's the platform. Anyone who buys it has Stephen's leverage over the entire SWS ecosystem.
- **Don't sign exclusivity** without a price that reflects exclusivity (10x non-exclusive minimum).
- **Don't talk to a patent monetization firm** before all other paths are exhausted. The phone call alone signals "patent troll" to anyone tracking him.
- **Don't go public without filing the provisionals first.** Once Astra Vault is publicly described, the 12-month USPTO clock starts.

## The contracts Stephen needs

Before any conversation that could lead to a deal:
1. **Mutual NDA template** — short, USPTO-acceptable, 2-year term. ~$0 (use Y Combinator template).
2. **License agreement template** for the SWS protocol (non-exclusive single-vertical). $2-5k from a patent licensing attorney.
3. **Term sheet** for a potential acquihire / acquisition. Hold off until a real conversation is on the table; ~$10-20k legal at that point.
4. **Founder shareholder agreement** if he raises capital. Standard YC docs work.

## Walk-away numbers

For any deal Stephen takes, he should know his walk-away number before the conversation. Anchored against `valuation-framework.md`:

- **Single-vertical SWS protocol license (non-exclusive):** walk away below $75k upfront.
- **Astra Vault app + application patents + LLC:** walk away below $3M total package.
- **Acquihire:** walk away if cash component < $500k or vesting > 4 years.
- **Patent #0 sale:** walk away. Don't sell.
