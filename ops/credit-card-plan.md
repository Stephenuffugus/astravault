# Credit Card Plan

What to apply for, in what order, with what spend strategy. Honest about
which cards are accessible at the current stage.

## Current stage: solo LLC, no revenue, personal credit only

Business credit cards that require business credit history or revenue are
**not accessible right now**:
- ❌ Brex (requires $50k+ deposit OR VC backing)
- ❌ Ramp (similar)
- ❌ Mercury (similar; also primarily a bank, not a card issuer)
- ❌ Capital One Spark Cash Plus (requires revenue, business credit)
- ❌ Most premium business cards (Amex Business Platinum, Chase Ink Business Premier)

Business credit cards that DO accept personal credit + LLC EIN:
- ✅ Chase Ink Business Unlimited — $0 annual fee, 1.5% cash back, common starter
- ✅ Chase Ink Business Cash — $0 fee, tiered 5% on office supplies
- ✅ Amex Blue Business Cash — $0 fee, 2% cash back up to $50k/yr
- ✅ Amex Blue Business Plus — $0 fee, 2x points up to $50k/yr
- ✅ Capital One Spark Cash Select (no annual fee version) — $0 fee, 1.5% cash back

## Recommended application order

Wait 90+ days between business card applications to avoid the "too many
inquiries" trigger on Chase / Amex risk models.

### Application #1 — Chase Ink Business Unlimited (recommended starter)

**Why:** $0 annual fee, accessible bar, useful 1.5% on everything, current
sign-up bonus is typically 60k-90k Ultimate Rewards points (~$600-$900
equivalent).

**Application requirements:**
- Personal credit score (FICO 680+ recommended)
- LLC EIN (you have this)
- Business name (your LLC's legal name)
- Estimated annual revenue (you can answer truthfully: "$0-$10k projected"; lenders accept low projections)
- Years in business (your LLC formation date)

**Use the sign-up bonus to:**
1. Pay 3 USPTO provisional patent filings ($960) — TIER 1 spend from `expense-ledger.md`
2. Pay Apple Developer Program annual fee ($99)
3. Hold remainder for emergencies

**Spend the minimum to trigger the bonus** (typically $3k-$5k in first 3
months). Pay off in full each month from any incoming revenue, never carry
a balance.

### Application #2 — Amex Blue Business Cash (in 90+ days)

**Why:** different network (Amex vs Visa), 2% cash back on everything up
to $50k/yr is better than Chase's 1.5%. Adds redundancy.

**Application requirements:** same shape as Chase, separate inquiry.

### Application #3 — Capital One Spark Cash Select (in another 90+ days)

**Why:** Capital One has different underwriting; useful if Chase / Amex
have hit their limits with you. 1.5% cash back, $0 fee.

## What to avoid

- **Don't apply for personal-name cards labeled "Business"** — some are
  consumer cards in disguise. The 3 above are legitimate business cards.
- **Don't max out a single card to fund a big spend** — keeps utilization
  ratio low (under 30%) preserves your credit score for the next
  application.
- **Don't take a cash advance.** Cash advance APR is 25%+ with no grace
  period and a 3-5% fee. Cheaper to wait for revenue.
- **Don't apply for retail-store cards** (Best Buy Business, etc). They
  don't help.

## When to upgrade to better cards

When YTD revenue crosses **$25k**: apply for Amex Business Gold or Amex
Business Platinum (3-4x points on relevant categories, but they have $295
and $695 annual fees that only make sense at scale).

When YTD revenue crosses **$100k**: apply for Brex or Ramp. Their value
prop is corporate cards with no personal guarantee, expense management
software, and integrations.

## What to do if Chase declines

This happens sometimes for LLCs without revenue. Options:
1. Try Amex Blue Business Cash first instead (Amex has different
   underwriting; sometimes more lenient on early-stage LLCs)
2. Apply for a personal credit card with strong cash-back categories
   (Citi Custom Cash, Wells Fargo Active Cash) and use it for business
   expenses — this is what most solo founders actually do
3. Try a Capital One Spark personal-guarantee card

## Don't forget

LLCs typically don't BUILD business credit unless they have a separate
EIN, DUNS number (free via Dun & Bradstreet), and vendor reporting to the
business credit bureaus. For your scale right now, this isn't worth the
overhead — your personal credit + LLC EIN is the right structure.
